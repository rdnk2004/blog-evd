Chapter 7

So Near, Yet Beyond My Reach

I followed Ram, unaware of my surroundings, carrying an emptiness that words could never describe.

We stopped in front of the NICU.

I had heard of an ICU before, but this was the first time I had heard the term NICU.

I did not even know what those letters meant.

I only knew that my baby was inside.

Only one person was allowed to enter.
So I had to go in alone.

A nurse asked me to wear a pair of slippers meant for use inside the unit and a gown over my clothes.
Everything felt unfamiliar.
Clinical.
Cold.

Inside, there were a few tiny beds, each surrounded by machines and monitors.
Some beds stood empty, while only three or four held little lives, each fighting its own silent battle.

The nurse pointed ahead and said,
"You can see your baby, but please don't touch him. Don't take much time."

She did not know that I had seen my baby only once before..
a blurred glimpse that had lasted only a few moments.
I could hardly recognize my own child.
What a plight for a mother.

Seven long days had passed as I waited for this moment.

And there I stood…..
my child and I in the same room, yet almost like strangers.

Not knowing which little baby was mine, I quietly asked the nurse,
"Which one is my baby?"

She pointed towards one of the beds.
I walked closer.
He was lying on his left side.

And then I saw him.

The first thing I noticed was not his face.

A long line of stitches ran beneath his tiny right arm, stretching from the side of his chest towards his back.
A drainage tube had been inserted just below it.

I stood there, frozen.

Was this what I had waited seven long days to see?

Something shattered within me.
But I held myself together.
I did not cry.
I did not touch him.
I simply stood there.

Both of us carried stitches upon our bodies, yet all I could feel was his pain within me.

I longed to touch him.
To hold him.
To look at him without fear.

Just then, he began to cry.
I did not know what to do.
The small gauze swab he had been sucking slipped from his mouth.

The nurse quickly picked it up and gently placed it back into his mouth.
Almost instantly, he settled down.
As though that tiny piece of gauze was the only comfort he could hold on to in his unfamiliar world.

I wanted to stay.
I wanted to stand beside him a little longer.
To memorize every tiny detail.
To pour out all the love in my heart without even touching him.

But I could not.

With one last look at my little warrior, I had to walk away.

Leaving that room was one of the hardest things I had ever done.

As I stepped outside, I felt completely drained.
I sat quietly on the steps nearby, trying to gather myself.

Outside the NICU, I noticed a few more anxious faces.
Parents carrying heavy hearts...
Perhaps hearts even heavier than ours.

Just then, the Chief paediatrician arrived for his morning rounds.
Ram and I waited outside.

As he came towards us, he gently offered words of hope and reassurance.

The surgeon had not yet arrived, so there was nothing more we could do but wait.

Soon, Ram had to take me back home before returning to the hospital once again.

And I left carrying only one thing with me..

the image of my little warrior...

stitched,

fragile,

fighting,

and far beyond the comfort of my embrace.

Even today, after twenty-two years, that memory has never faded.

The pain remains untouched by time.

Some moments in life do not belong to the past.

They continue to live quietly within us...
forever.
